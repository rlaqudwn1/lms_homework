# NEXT SAVE — backlog service landscape

Checked 2026-07-23. This is a qualitative product-positioning comparison, not a
market-size or revenue analysis.

| Service | Operating-page promise | Primary job | NEXT SAVE distinction |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Backloggd](https://backloggd.com/?lang=en) | Track a game collection with statuses, time, journals, reviews, lists, and friend activity | Record and express a gaming journey | Reduce the immediate choice to three explained candidates |
| [Grouvee](https://www.grouvee.com/) | Organize games into shelves, import a Steam library, review games, and participate in community lists/activity | Organize and share a collection | Avoid asking the user to maintain another shelf or list |
| [HowLongToBeat](https://howlongtobeat.com/) | Compare completion-time estimates and manage a backlog | Add time context to planning | Combine a disclosed taste interpretation with a short decision set |

Supporting implementation example:
[Xbox added HowLongToBeat estimates to PC game detail pages](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2022/09/14/september-updates-xbox-app-on-pc/)
to make choosing the next game easier. This supports time context as a useful
decision signal; it does not validate NEXT SAVE's recommendation hypothesis.

## Observed category pattern

The compared services emphasize one or more of collection tracking, statuses,
journaling, reviews, social activity, custom lists, Steam import, and completion
time. They already serve those jobs; NEXT SAVE should not claim that tracking is
missing from the market.

## Opportunity hypothesis

People with a large library may need help completing a narrower job after
tracking and organization: deciding what to start tonight. NEXT SAVE therefore
tests whether a disclosed taste summary plus exactly three explained candidates
reduces decision effort.

This is a hypothesis, not evidence of demand. No traffic, user-count, revenue, or
market-size claim is made.
