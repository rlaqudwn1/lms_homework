## Product brief

- One-sentence idea: A decision-support demo that turns a large Steam backlog into a taste atlas and three explainable next-game picks.
- Primary user: A time-poor adult Steam player with roughly 100–500 owned games who repeatedly scrolls without choosing what to play.
- User problem: Existing libraries and trackers return another list; the user needs a small decision with a reason they can trust.
- One core flow: Open landing page → enter a Steam profile URL → load disclosed demo data → inspect taste atlas → review three recommendations and their evidence.
- Must-have screens: landing/input, atlas result, recommendation detail.
- Explicit non-goals: live Steam integration, production authentication, social feed, reviews, payments, cross-platform libraries, and a production recommendation model.

## Design input

- Preserve: ink-navy stage, restrained cool UI, Pretendard typography, teal primary accent, atlas-specific semantic colors, evidence-first copy, and game art as the main source of saturated color.
- Responsive requirement: two-column regions collapse to one column; atlas and recommendation cards remain readable at 360 px.
- Accessibility constraints: keyboard-visible focus, labelled URL input, non-color status labels, sufficient contrast, reduced-motion support, and useful alt text.

## Track decision

- Proposed track: `course-mock` (reusable course slice).
- Why: The PRD is deliberately at `Pivot`, the riskiest sharing assumption is unvalidated, and the LMS needs a demonstrable React flow sooner than a production system.
- Mock boundary: seeded profiles and deterministic recommendation fixtures. Every result surface must display `예시 데이터 · 데모`.
- Promotion gate: do not add live Steam data, auth, or social features until the owner explicitly promotes the project and records data/privacy decisions.
